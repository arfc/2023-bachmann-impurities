\begin{abstract}
Many new advanced reactor designs require fuel enriched 
between 5-20\% $^{235}$U. To assist in producing 
fuel at these enrichment levels, government-owned 
stockpiles of highly-enriched uranium can be downblended. However, 
fuel produced from these stockpiles contain uranium 
impurities that are not often found when enriching natural 
uranium or accounted for when modeling reactor cores. To 
address this concern, this work models the Xe-100 and 
Micro Modular Reactor 
designs and compares their performance with  
fuel containing only $^{235}$U and $^{238}$U to fuel from downblended 
highly enriched uranium.We compare the models based on 
the \keff, \betaEff, energy- and spatially dependent neutron 
flux, and the fuel, coolant, moderator, and total reactivity 
temperature feedback coefficients. The results 
show that the fuel from downblended highly enriched uranium 
stockpiles leads to differences in each of the metrics. 
However, the differences are not always statistically significant 
or would not lead to prevent the ability to reach key design 
parameters, such as cycle length. 
\end{abstract}
