\section{Conclusions}
This work looks at the effects of using 
\gls*{HALEU} downblended from \gls*{HEU} inventories on select 
\gls*{HTGR} models to investigate if these 
effects can significantly impact 
reactor performance. Three different \gls*{HALEU} 
compositions were used in reactor physics simulations of 
an Xe-100-like (the Sangamon200) core and an \gls*{MMR}-like 
core. The \gls*{HALEU} 
compositions were based on pure \gls*{HALEU} (only $^{235}$U and 
$^{238}$U), downblended \gls*{HEU} from \gls*{EBR} inventories, 
and downblended \gls*{HEU} inventories from the Y-12 National Security Complex. 
The reactor performance metrics in this work included
the effective neutron multiplication factor (\keff), 
effective delayed neutron fraction (\betaEff), energy- and 
spatially-dependent 
neutron flux, and the fuel, moderator, coolant, and total reactivity 
temperature feedback coefficients ($\alpha_F$, $\alpha_M$,
$\alpha_C$, $\alpha_T$). The Sangamon200 model was 
at an equilibrium state and the \gls*{MMR} model was modeled 
at \gls*{BOL}, \gls*{MOL}, and \gls*{EOL} states. 

In the Sangamon200 model, the impure fuel compositions resulted 
in larger \keff values, which suggests that additional neutron 
poisons may be needed during operation to control the reactivity 
in the core. The \betaEff values when using the impure fuel are 
smaller than the \betaEff from the pure fuel, which suggests that 
reactivity control mechanisms may not be as effective when using 
the impure fuels. The impure fuels resulted in a depression in the 
thermal neutron flux across the active region of the core.  Finally, the impure fuels 
lead to reactivity feedback coefficients that are of a greater magnitude 
or are within error of the values from the pure fuel.
Therefore, the effects of the impure fuel compositions 
may also affect reactor operation parameters. 

In the \gls*{MMR}-like model, the impure fuels result in smaller 
\keff values at each burnup step, but do not prevent the reactor 
from still being critical at \gls*{EOL} and achieving a 20 year 
cycle time. The \betaEff values from 
the different \gls*{HALEU} compositions are all within error 
of each other or the impure fuels result in larger 
\betaEff values at each burnup step. This result suggests that the fuel 
composition does not significantly impact this metric for this 
reactor design or will enable neutron control mechanisms to 
be more impactful. The impure fuels lead to larger fluxes in the 
fast energy range, compared with the pure fuel. The differences 
in the radial flux are dependent on proximity to fuel pin 
and coolant channels in the core, with the differences being 
larger near a coolant channel. 
Most of the reactivity feedback 
coefficients from the different fuel compositions are within 
error of each other, especially at the \gls*{EOL} burnup 
step. 

The results presented in this work 
suggest that these three different \gls*{HALEU} compositions 
can have a significant effect on many of the performance metrics of these 
two reactor designs, but would not necessarily prevent them from operating in 
a safe state. The largest changes from the different fuel 
compositions were observed in the spatially-dependent fluxes, 
which could affect material degradation in the reactor. 


\subsection{Future Work}
The work presented here provides a quantitative baseline to 
gauge the impacts 
of using downblended \gls*{HEU} in two \gls*{HTGR} designs. 
This 
work compared the reactor physics parameters when using 
three different uranium isotopic compositions. While the 
results here provide valuable insights, a more comprehensive 
sensitivity analysis could extend these findings. Such 
a study could use more fuel compositions in the reactor 
models by either modifying the three compositions used 
in this work (e.g., applying 5-10\% variations in each 
uranium isotope) or apply a systematic grid of uranium 
compositions. These finer detail sensitivity studies 
could elucidate the effect of each isotope on the reactor 
physics parameters explored. 

Additionally, 
one may find that higher fidelity models reveal further insights into 
these effects. Improving the model fidelity may 
include updating the reactor geometries, using 
fuel pebbles with explicitly defined fuel kernels and at 
least one representative outer \gls*{TRISO} layer (i.e., a 
heterogeneous pebble model), 
modeling temperature distributions across the core, and 
updating the material compositions to include any 
burnable absorbers or other neutron poisons that would be 
used during operation to control reactivity. Notably, 
analysis of a heterogeneous \gls*{TRISO} pebble 
can provide more insight into the magnitude of the effect 
of pebble homogenization on the results in this work. 

Finally, the Sangamon200 model assumes an equilibrium state. 
However, downblended \gls*{HEU} may be used to create fuel for start up 
cores. Therefore, analysis of these compositions in an 
Xe-100-like 
design in a start up core configuration would be more beneficial to 
understanding potential limitations of these fuel compositions 
to initially fuel reactors while developing infrastructure to 
enrich natural uranium to produce \gls*{HALEU}.
