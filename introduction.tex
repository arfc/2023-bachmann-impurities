\section{Introduction}
The US nuclear industry is looking to deploy a suite of advanced reactors. 
These reactors will have notable benefits of improved safety, longer cycle 
times, and greater flexibility compared with the current fleet of \glspl{LWR}. 
However, to support the deployment of these reactors the US needs to 
develop new supply chains for fresh fuel. Many advanced reactors will 
require \gls{HALEU}, or uranium enriched between 5-20\% $^{235}$U, while 
the current \gls{LWR} fleet requires fuel enriched to less than 5\% 
$^{235}$U. 

To develop the supply chain of \gls{HALEU}, the US \gls{DOE} has proposed 
the enrichment of natural uranium and the downblending of \gls{DOE}-owned 
\gls{HEU} stockpiles \cite{griffith_overview_2020}. Based on published 
isotopic compositions \cite{vaden_isotopic_2018,nelson_foreign_2010}, 
\gls{HALEU} created from downblending these stockpiles will have impurities 
of other uranium isotopes. Notably, $^{234}$U and $^{236}$U are present, 
which are known neutron poisons and can affect the neutronics of a 
reactor. 

Previous analysis of downblended \gls{HEU} in a NIST research reactor 
\cite{celikten_effects_2021} shows a 0.43\% drop in reactivity and 
an 8\% reduction in the cycle length when using fuel downblended from 
stockpiles at the Y-12 National Security Complex. Even when the \gls{HALEU} 
is fabricated by enriching natural uranium the range of possible impurity 
concentrations has been shown to affect the $k_{eff}$ value of the MIT 
Reactor by about 280 pcm \cite{mascolino_impact_2022}. The results of these 
previous analyses are relatively small changes to the reactor performance, 
but they are only performed on research reactors. Research reactors are 
typically a smaller power output and core size than a commercial power 
reactor. Therefore, the effects of impurities in \gls{HALEU} may be more 
pronounced in a commercial reactor.

The purpose of this work is to investigate how the impurities in \gls{HALEU} 
produced from downblending \gls{HEU} can affect the neutronics and operation 
of select advanced reactors intended for commercial operation.
