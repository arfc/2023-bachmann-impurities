\section{Introduction}
Nuclear energy critically underpins the United States 
energy mix and 
will likely be needed to help meet future energy needs. 
The current 
fleet of US commercial reactors are \glspl*{LWR}, 
which require uranium enriched to less than 5\% $^{235}$U
based on \gls*{NRC} regulations \cite{noauthor_10_2006}.
But advanced reactors designs being commercialized 
today  require 
uranium enriched to between 5-20\% $^{235}$U, or \gls*{HALEU}. 
Using \gls*{HALEU} in reactors, including in \glspl*{LWR}, 
helps in achieving higher burnups and longer cycle 
times \cite{carlson_implications_2022}.
However, there is currently no commercial-scale infrastructure to 
produce \gls*{HALEU} in the US right now. 

There are two primary methods for producing \gls*{HALEU}. The first 
option is to enrich natural uranium to the desired level. Developing 
infrastructure to enable this \gls*{HALEU} production method 
is underway 
\cite{nuclear_energy_institute_establishing_2022,us_nuclear_regulatory_commission_centrus_2021},
but will take time. 
The other \gls*{HALEU} production option is to downblend \gls*{HEU} to 
the desired \gls*{HALEU}
level. The \gls*{DOE} is actively downblending various \gls*{HEU} inventories
to create \gls*{HALEU}: spent fuel from \gls*{EBR} and \gls*{HEU} solution 
at \gls*{SRS} \cite{nuclear_energy_institute_establishing_2022}. The 
spent fuel from \gls*{EBR} can produce a total of 10 metric ton uranium (MTU) 
of \gls*{HALEU} at 19.75\% enrichment 
\cite{nuclear_energy_institute_establishing_2022}. 
Estimates of the \gls*{HALEU} from the stored \gls*{HEU} solution 
at \gls*{SRS} vary from 4 MTU \cite{nuclear_energy_institute_establishing_2022} 
to 20 MTU \cite{regalbuto_addressing_2020}.
These quantities are much less than potential \gls*{HALEU} demand 
to support a fleet of \gls*{HALEU}-fueled reactors 
\cite{dixon_estimated_2022,bachmann_enrichment_2021}, but the \gls*{HEU} 
sources can be used to provide fuel for demonstration 
projects or initial core loadings while developing infrastructure 
to enrich natural uranium. 

In addition to the limited quantities of \gls*{HEU} available for 
downblending, the \gls*{HALEU} produced from downblended \gls*{HEU}
has uranium impurities that are not typically included in normal 
fuel loadings \cite{vaden_isotopic_2018,nelson_foreign_2010}, 
and are thus not included in reactor physics analysis of the fuel. 
Other uranium isotopes present in \gls*{HALEU} from downblended 
\gls*{HEU} includes
$^{234}$U and $^{236}$U, which are neutron poisons and 
can affect reactor neutronics. 
\gls*{HALEU} produced through enriching natural uranium for use 
in the \gls*{MIT} Reactor 
contained measurable concentrations of $^{233}$U, $^{234}$U, $^{235}$U,
$^{236}$U, and $^{238}$U \cite{mascolino_impact_2022}.
Variations in the concentrations of these isotopes affected 
the \keff of the reactor by up to 280 pcm 
\cite{mascolino_impact_2022}. 
This is a relatively small change in \keff, but the 
results show that different concentrations of different uranium 
isotopes affect reactor performance. 
Other work analyzed the effects of the uranium composition 
for a potential research reactor at the \gls*{NIST}, comparing the 
performance of nominally enriched \gls*{HALEU} (i.e., containing 
only $^{235}$U and $^{238}$U) with 
\gls*{HALEU} from downblended \gls*{HEU} from the Y-12 National 
Security Complex \cite{celikten_effects_2021} at the same 
enrichment level. This analysis 
showed a 0.43\% drop in reactivity, which led to an 8\% 
reduction in cycle length for the reactor 
\cite{celikten_effects_2021}.
This analysis further shows how the impurities from downblended 
\gls*{HEU} can affect reactor performance. 

These previous investigations were both performed based on research 
reactor designs, which are typically much smaller in power and 
size than commercial reactors. Research reactors also have 
substantially different reactor physics behavior than commercial 
designs. Therefore, if downblended 
\gls*{HEU} is to be used in commercial reactors there is a 
need for analysis
on how these impurities may impact performance of these designs. 
This paper will investigate how the 
impurities in \gls*{HALEU} produced from downblending \gls*{HEU} can 
affect the neutronics and operation of select advanced reactors 
intended for commercial operation. This work compares the effects of 
the impurities based on the effective neutron 
multiplication factor 
(\keff), effective delayed neutron fractions (\betaEff), 
energy- and 
spatially-dependent neutron flux ($\phi$), as well as the fuel, 
coolant, 
moderator, and total reactivity temperature feedback coefficients
($\alpha_F$, $\alpha_C$, $\alpha_M$, $\alpha_T$). In some cases, 
the compounding effect of impurities and burnup on these 
factors was also compared. 
This analysis will provide 
insight into how the impurities in the \gls*{HALEU} fuel affects 
the reactors, and if the impurities are prohibitive to
safe reactor operation.
